The house

Leave the cityat the shore,come back slower

Brume began with a smoke sauna, a jetty and one rule: no phones past the changing room. Ten years on the rule has not changed. You move between heat and cold water until your head goes quiet, then rest by the stove and watch the mist lift off the lake.

How the house was built

The round

One round,three temperatures

Bathers here follow an old order. Repeat it three or four times; nobody keeps count but you.

  1. Step 1

    Heat

    Twelve minutes on the upper bench.

    Sit high and breathe slowly. Pour water on the stones when the room asks for it, and let the heat climb until your shoulders drop.

    8–15 min · 80–95 °C

  2. Step 2

    Cold

    Twenty steps to the lake.

    Walk out along the jetty and go in to the shoulders. For one minute the cold shuts every thought out; that is the point of it.

    1–2 min · 4–16 °C

  3. Step 3

    Rest

    As long as it takes.

    Wrap up by the stove, drink something warm and say nothing. The calm that arrives now is the reason you came.

    15–20 min · by the window

Löyly

Water on the stones. A breath of heat rolls off the ceiling and the room goes quiet.

In Finnish this breath is called löyly. Our sauna masters pour every twenty minutes, with birch, mint or tar, and nobody speaks while it settles.

The lake

The fifth roomhas no walls

Ellery Water is fed by springs and stays cold even in August. A boardwalk crosses the reeds to our jetty, the ladder goes down three and a half metres, and from December a stove burns at the end of the ice channel.

  • Boardwalk to the jetty, sixty-four metres
  • Ladder and rope line out to the buoys
  • Ice channel from December to March
  • A lifeguard on the jetty at weekends

The house

A house builtfor the weather

Charred larch outside, alder and aspen inside, glass wherever the lake is. The house sits low on its stilts so the mist rolls under it, and every bench in it faces the water.

We heat with wood from our own forest, and rain from the roof fills the cold tubs. In winter the snow on the roof tells you which room is hottest.

Questions

Beforeyour first round

Do I need to book?

Weekends and evenings fill up, so book ahead. On weekday mornings you can usually walk in; ask at the desk.

What should I bring?

Nothing at all. A robe, towels, slippers and a locker are included. Bring a swimsuit if you prefer one for the lake.

Is bathing mixed?

Yes, every day except Tuesday evenings, which are for women only. Swimwear or a towel in all shared rooms.

I am new to saunas. Where do I start?

Begin in the herb steam and keep the first rounds short. A host walks first-timers through the round at ten and at three.

Can I come if I am pregnant or have a heart condition?

Please ask your doctor first. The steam room and the rest room are gentle; the smoke sauna and the lake are not.

Do you sell gift cards?

Yes, for any pass, by email or at the desk. They are valid for a year from the day you buy them.
