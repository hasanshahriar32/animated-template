From wet clay to a loud floor.

  1. Step 1

    Press

    White stoneware clay is pressed into a steel frame, one tile at a time, and trimmed square while it is still soft.

  2. Step 2

    Dry

    Ten days on open racks. The clay loses its water slowly and evenly, so the tile stays flat and the edges stay sharp.

  3. Step 3

    Pour the frame

    The frame glaze goes on through a mask. Raw, it looks chalky and pale; nobody can tell Signal from Raspberry yet.

  4. Step 4

    Set the core

    The core glaze is laid in a second pass, with the ring left bare. Dot, square or full: the mask decides how much shows.

  5. Step 5

    Fire

    Twelve hours up to 1,200 °C and a day to cool. The kiln turns chalk into colour, and the bare ring comes out white.

Write to us
Can Inset tiles go outside?

Yes. Stoneware fired at 1,200 °C takes frost and sun. For outdoor floors and pool edges we fire the R11 non-slip finish, which mutes the glaze a little; ask for it with your samples.

How much should I order?

Your area plus 10% for cuts, 15% for a diagonal or a room with many corners. Every batch is fired once, so order the spares now: a later firing can differ by a shade.

How long does an order take?

Three to five weeks depending on the size, from the day you approve the proof tile. Delivery in Spain takes two days, across Europe four to six by pallet.

Can you mix a glaze that is not on the list?

For orders over 40 m² we match a colour you send us. It takes three test firings and adds two weeks; the glaze then joins your account for later orders.

How do I clean them?

Warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner. The glaze is sealed by the firing; the bare ring is vitrified too, so it needs no sealant and does not stain.

Do you lay the tiles?

No, but we send a laying plan with every order and work with tilers in Valencia, Barcelona and Madrid whom we can recommend.
