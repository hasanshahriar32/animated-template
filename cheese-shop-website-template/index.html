What the cellar does

Cheese leaves the farm young. The last weeks or months decide how it tastes, and they happen forty steps below the shop.

  1. Step 1

    Arrive

    Wheels come in from the makers at a few weeks old and rest for a day before anyone touches them.

  2. Step 2

    Wash and brush

    Washed rinds get a brine wipe twice a week; natural rinds are brushed so the moulds stay even.

  3. Step 3

    Turn

    Every Thursday each wheel is turned by hand, so the moisture stays even through the paste.

  4. Step 4

    Taste with an iron

    We pull a core with a cheese iron, taste it and put the plug back. Nothing goes upstairs before it’s right.

  5. Step 5

    Bring it up

    Ready wheels go up to the counter, where they are cut to order and wrapped in waxed paper.

Questions at the counter

Anything else, ask us in the shop or by email.

  • Do you post cheese?

    Yes: Tuesday to Thursday, by next-day courier in insulated boxes, anywhere in mainland UK. We don’t post on Fridays, so nothing sits in a depot over the weekend.

  • How long will it keep?

    Hard cheeses keep two to three weeks in their paper in the salad drawer; soft and fresh cheeses about a week. Re-wrap in fresh paper if the old one turns greasy.

  • Can you cut a whole wheel for a wedding?

    Gladly: stacked wheels, whole cheeses and long boards for any number of guests. Give us two weeks’ notice for the big wheels.

  • Is it all raw-milk cheese?

    Most of it, and every label says which. If you’re pregnant or avoiding raw milk, ask and we’ll point you to the pasteurised ones.

  • Do you have vegetarian cheeses?

    About half the counter uses vegetarian rennet, and the label strip says so.

  • Can I bring my own containers?

    Yes. We weigh your box first and cut straight into it.
