Mud to table in six steps

Every Fettle piece goes through the same six steps and two firings. It takes about three weeks, and none of it can be hurried.

  1. Step 1

    Wedge

    Each block is kneaded on a canvas bench to push out trapped air, so nothing cracks or bursts in the kiln.

  2. Step 2

    Throw

    The clay is centred on the wheel, opened and pulled up into a cylinder, then shaped into a bowl, cup or plate.

  3. Step 3

    Trim

    Leather-hard pieces go back on the wheel upside down to cut a clean foot ring and lose extra weight.

  4. Step 4

    Bisque

    A first, slow firing to 1000°C turns dry clay into porous bisque that can soak up glaze.

  5. Step 5

    Glaze

    Pieces are dipped by hand in one of our house glazes; each foot is waxed so it never sticks to the kiln shelf.

  6. Step 6

    Fire

    The glaze firing runs overnight to 1240°C. We open the kiln the next afternoon, never sooner.

Four glazes, mixed in the back room

We weigh and sieve every bucket ourselves. Each glaze is food safe, tested in the dishwasher and fired on our own clay body.

  • Cobalt

    A deep, glossy blue that breaks pale where it thins over the rims. The glaze most of our restaurant plates wear.

    Gloss · dinner plates, bowls

  • Iron red

    Rust that turns orange on edges and almost black where it pools. Every piece comes out of the kiln a little different.

    Satin · vases, serving dishes

  • Celadon

    A pale green-grey you can see the clay through, glassy and calm. Teacups, rice bowls and anything for a slow breakfast.

    Gloss · cups, small bowls

  • Tenmoku

    Near-black brown with a bronze shine on the shoulders. Heavy, quiet and the best background for food we know.

    Gloss · sharing plates, jugs

Questions

+
Do I need any experience for a class?+

None at all. The taster evening and the six-week course both start from the very beginning, and the groups are small enough that everyone gets help on the wheel.

What should I wear?+

Clothes you do not mind getting clay on, and short nails if you can. We lend aprons and towels; clay washes out of cotton easily.

When can I collect what I made?+

Three to four weeks after your class, once the pieces have been trimmed, bisque fired, glazed and fired again. We email you when they are on the collection shelf.

Is your tableware dishwasher safe?+

Yes. All four house glazes are food safe and tested in commercial dishwashers and microwaves. Unglazed outsides are best washed by hand.

How big does a commission have to be?+

We start at about 40 pieces of one shape. Samples take four weeks and a full service ten to twelve, and we keep your glaze recipe on file for reorders.

Do you sell gift vouchers?+

For any class or any amount. Email us and we will send a printable voucher the same day; it is valid for a year.
