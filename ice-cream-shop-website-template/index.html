So what happens before noon?

Plums are becoming sorbet the coldest way to eat October.

Melty is a twelve-seat parlour on Example Street. Every morning we cook whatever the market had into twelve small batches, and when a tub is scraped clean, that flavour is done until next year.

Before you’re up, we’re at it.

At six we buy what the market has, cook it into a base , churn it cold and scoop it by noon.

  1. 1

    Market at six

    Stall 14 keeps the ripest, ugliest fruit aside for us. Ugly is sweeter.

  2. 2

    Cook the base

    Milk from Hollow Lane, warmed with the fruit or nuts until it smells like the flavour should.

  3. 3

    Churn it cold

    One small machine, six litres at a time, twelve runs before eleven.

  4. 4

    Rest till noon

    Two hours at minus fourteen so it scoops clean. Then the shutter goes up.

Asked on every hot Saturday:

Will it survive the walk home?

Pints leave in a lined paper bag that keeps them firm for forty minutes. Going further? Ask for a block of dry ice at the till. It’s free, and it fizzes.

Asked by a very polite eight-year-old:

Is there one for my dairy‑free friend?

Always at least four. Our sorbets are fruit, sugar and water, nothing else, and the oat-milk chocolate runs on its own churn on Tuesdays and Fridays so nothing crosses over.

Asked every week in May:

Can we bring the whole birthday?

Up to twenty of you on weekday afternoons. Everyone gets a tasting flight of six, and the birthday person names a scoop for the board. Book a week ahead, right here.
