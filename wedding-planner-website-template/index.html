Every wedding starts with a flower in season

We build each day from what our growers are cutting that month, then let the flower choose the linen, the light and the hour of the ceremony.

March to May

Narcissus, fritillary, apple blossom, early ranunculus.

Pale linen, bare tables and a ceremony at noon, when the light is kind and the orchard is still cold in the shade.

Spring

June to August

Garden roses, sweet peas, cornflowers, scabious.

Long tables outside, dinner at eight, and the first dance timed to the last of the light at half past nine.

Summer

September to November

Dahlias, rose hips, amaranth, the last of the sweet peas.

Orchards heavy with fruit, a ceremony at two, candles lit by five and a fire pit for the speeches.

Autumn

December to February

Amaryllis, hellebores, forced narcissus, evergreens.

Glasshouses warmed for the day, dark red on white cloth, and everyone inside by four with something hot.

From first light to the last song

An aubade is a song for lovers at dawn. Our wedding days start there: we are on site before sunrise, leave after the last guest, and in between the day is written down to the minute.

  1. 05:40

    First light

    We arrive with the growers' buckets and the coffee. The garden is still grey and nobody is awake but the birds.

  2. 08:00

    Flowers in

    Arches, tables and the bouquet are built on site from what was cut yesterday evening.

  3. 11:30

    Getting ready

    Hair, make-up and a quiet hour. One of us is with you, the other is with everyone else.

  4. 14:00

    The ceremony

    Chairs dried of dew, the order of service on every seat, the rain plan folded in a pocket.

  5. 15:00

    On the terrace

    Drinks under the trees, the photographer's list ticked off, grandparents in the shade.

  6. 18:30

    Dinner under glass

    Candles lit before anyone sits down, speeches between courses, timed with the kitchen.

  7. 20:40

    Golden hour

    Ten minutes in the field for the photographs that end up on the wall.

  8. 22:30

    The last song

    Sparklers, taxis at the gate, and the two of us packing the candles away after midnight.

In their words

Questions couples ask first

If yours is not here, ask it on the first call. It is free, it takes half an hour, and nobody will try to sell you a package.

How far ahead should we book?

For full planning, twelve to eighteen months. For the day itself, eight weeks is enough, though summer Saturdays are usually taken a year ahead.

Do you only plan garden weddings?

Mostly gardens, orchards and glasshouses within ninety minutes of Bath. We have also planned barns, a boathouse and one library. Ask us.

What happens if it rains?

Every wedding has a written rain plan by the third month: a covered place for the ceremony, umbrellas in the right colour, and a time on the day at which we decide.

Can we bring our own suppliers?

Of course. We meet them, share the run sheet and make sure they know where to park the van and when the speeches start.

How many weddings do you take?

Eighteen a year and never two on one weekend, so both of us can be at yours from first light.

What does it cost?

Full planning starts at £7,400, partial planning at £4,200 and the day itself at £1,900. After the first call you get a written quote with nothing left out.
