Six rooms, one team

For anyone with a straightforward pregnancy, booked from eight weeks.

No wards. No strangers at the door.

Linnea is run by midwives, not a hospital rota. You meet your team of four at booking, you labour in a room that is yours alone, and one of those four faces is there when your baby arrives.

Your pregnancy with us

From the first phone call to the day labour starts, the same four midwives look after you: at home, or here with the kettle on.

Refer yourself

  1. From 8 weeks

    Call us, or fill in the form

    No doctor’s letter needed. We ask about your health and any earlier births, and tell you which team will be yours.

  2. 10 weeks

    Booking visit, at home

    Two unhurried hours with one of your four midwives, on your own sofa.

  3. 16 to 31 weeks

    Four checks at the house

    Blood pressure, growth, your baby’s heartbeat and whatever is on your mind, in the front lounge rather than a clinic.

  4. 34 weeks

    A birth-plan evening

    You, your birth partner and your midwife, in the room you would like, talking through what you want and what you don’t.

  5. 37 weeks on

    Ready when you are

    Call the labour line at any hour, and a midwife who knows you answers.

On the day, one rhythm

Labour moves in phases. This is what happens in each one, where you are, and who is beside you.

  1. Phase 1 of 5

    Early labour

    Stay at home while it feels right. Call the labour line: your midwife listens to a few contractions with you and helps you judge when to come in.

    At home, on the phone

  2. Phase 2 of 5

    Active labour

    Your room is warm and the pool is filling as you arrive. From now on your midwife stays in the room, quietly checking on you and your baby.

    Your room, the pool filled

  3. Phase 3 of 5

    Transition

    The hardest stretch, and usually the shortest. Expect doubts, and expect your midwife to tell you that you are nearly there, because you are.

    Low light, steady voices

  4. Phase 4 of 5

    Birth

    In the water, on the stool, kneeling at the bed: wherever your body chooses. A second midwife joins quietly for the birth.

    Your position, your pace

  5. Phase 5 of 5

    The first hour

    Skin to skin, and nobody rushes. Your baby finds a first feed, the cord is cut when it is ready, and someone brings tea and toast.

    Skin to skin, tea and toast

The first six weeks, at home

  • Day 1
  • Day 3
  • Day 5
  • Day 10
  • Feeding café, Thursdays
  • Week 6 goodbye

We come to you. On the first day, the third and the fifth, a midwife from your team knocks on your door, weighs your baby, watches a feed and asks how you are, and really asks.

On day ten we check the weight again and do the heel-prick test. Then the visits thin out and phone calls take over, with the feeding café on Thursdays if you want company.

At six weeks we say goodbye, hand you to your health visitor with everything written down, and hope to see you at the café anyway.

Questions we hear at every tour

Ask yours on a tour, or call us on 0131 000 0000 during office hours.

Who can give birth at Linnea?

Anyone expecting one baby, head down, between 37 and 42 weeks, with a straightforward pregnancy. If something changes, we plan the next steps together and you keep your team.

Does it cost anything?

No. Care at Linnea is free for families in north Edinburgh, funded like any other maternity service. Only baby massage has a fee.

What pain relief can I have?

Warm water, massage, movement, TENS, and gas and air. If you want an epidural, we take you across to the obstetric unit, and your midwife comes with you.

What happens if I need a hospital?

The obstetric unit is ten minutes away. About one in five first labours here move across, mostly for a long labour or an epidural. Your midwife travels with you and stays.

Can my partner stay overnight?

Yes. Every room has a double bed, and Ember has a fold-out bed for long labours.

Can I bring my older children?

Yes, with another adult to look after them. The siblings’ room has toys, books and its own door to the garden.

How soon do we go home?

Usually four to six hours after the birth, once you have fed, showered and rested. You are welcome to stay the night.

Come and see the rooms

Tours run on Tuesday evenings and Saturday mornings and take about forty minutes. Partners and children are welcome. You can refer yourself on the same form.
