Every line runs the same way

We leave Florence on a Monday morning by regional train. Three towns, a day and a half in each, a working studio opened for us at every one, family-run rooms and long dinners. Back on platform three on Friday afternoon.

Twelve seats
One carriage section booked for the group. Never a coach.
One host
A Florentine who rides with you from the first platform to the last.
Three studios
A morning at the bench with the person who works there.

The Tuscan line

Florence to Prato, Carrara and San Miniato, and back to Florence. Each stop opens when the train gets there.

  1. Stop 1 · Monday 09:41

    Prato

    The wool mill. Old coats have been carded and re-spun here since 1890; Elena opens the carding room and an archive of forty thousand swatches kept in shoe boxes.

  2. Stop 2 · Tuesday 14:20

    Carrara

    The quarry and the bench. Up to the Colonnata faces by jeep, then down to Marco's studio, where you rough out a small block of your own.

  3. Stop 3 · Thursday 10:05

    San Miniato

    The leather bench. Giulia makes twenty pairs of shoes a year; you trace, cut and edge a card wallet that goes home in your pocket.

Who waits at each stop

Not guides: people at work, who let twelve strangers into the room for a morning and keep working while they explain.

  • Elena Mati

    Mill archivist · Prato

    “Every swatch in here was cut from a coat somebody wore out. I can tell you whose.”

  • Marco Bertozzi

    Stone carver · Carrara

    “The block tells you where it wants to split. I just listen a little faster than most.”

  • Giulia Ferro

    Shoemaker · San Miniato

    “A pair takes me forty hours. You will see the first four, and they are the important ones.”

A day on the line

  1. 07:52

    Platform three

    Coffee standing at the station bar, then the regional train out of Santa Maria Novella. Seats facing each other, the hills in the window, your host reading out the stops.

  2. 10:00

    Inside the studio

    Three hours at the bench with your hands on the material, one hour of questions, then lunch at the long table under the pergola.

  3. 20:30

    The town at dinner

    A trattoria your host has known for twenty years, a walk back through the lanes, and nobody has to drive.

Before you book

The questions people ask us on the phone, answered the way we answer them there.

How fit do I need to be?

Walks stay under five kilometres a day, mostly on cobbles and some steps. The Carrara quarry is reached by jeep.

What happens to my luggage?

One case each travels by van between hotels, so on the train you only carry a day bag.

Can I come on my own?

About half our travellers do. A single room costs €340 more and we never pair strangers.

What if a train is late or cancelled?

Your host rebooks on the spot. For strike days we keep a minibus and driver on call.

Are the studios step-free?

Two of the three on each line are. Call us before booking and we will tell you exactly what to expect.

How do I pay, and can I cancel?

A 20% deposit holds a seat and the rest is due sixty days before departure. Until then you can cancel for a full refund.
