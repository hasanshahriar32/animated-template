Four Ridges,
One Rule

Every Farlight stay sits on high ground with nothing between the bed and the horizon. Six to twelve stays a ridge, never more, and never in sight of each other.

  1. Eastern Sierra, California · 2,450 m

    Snowline

    Black timber cabins on a snowfield under the range. Wood stoves, deep baths and skis by the door from December to April.

    • 12 stays
    • 3 h 40 from Reno

  2. Southern Utah · 1,900 m

    Red Bench

    Glass domes on a sandstone shelf, red rock below and snow on the peaks behind. The darkest sky of the four, and the quietest.

    • 8 stays
    • 2 h 50 from St. George

  3. Great Basin, Nevada · 1,700 m

    Salt Rim

    Shell pods along the edge of an old lakebed. Flat to every horizon, so the sky starts at your feet and the sunsets last twice as long.

    • 10 stays
    • 3 h from Las Vegas

  4. Northern Arizona · 2,100 m

    Juniper Mesa

    Canvas suites among junipers and yucca, a short walk from the rim. The mildest winters of the four and the best ridge for families.

    • 8 stays
    • 1 h 30 from Flagstaff

One Night,
Five Lights

A night on a ridge is not dark all at once. It goes in stages, and we built the stay around them, from the hour you arrive to the coffee on the deck.

  1. Arrival, around four

    Last light

    Your host meets you at the gate, walks your bags up and lights the stove. You get keys, a map and no wifi password.

  2. The sky goes blue

    Blue hour

    The ridge goes quiet and the first planets come out. The best twenty minutes for photographs and for not taking any.

  3. Supper outside

    Firelight

    The supper box arrives with the wood. Grill on the fire pit, bake in the coals, keep the blanket close.

  4. Lamps off at ten

    Starlight

    Give your eyes twenty minutes. Then the Milky Way needs no help, and the telescope on the deck is yours until morning.

  5. Coffee on the deck

    First light

    The ridge turns pink before anything else does. Breakfast comes up at eight; checkout is when you are ready.

We Keep
It Dark

A dark sky is the one thing we could never build back. These rules hold on every ridge, and they are written into our leases with the land.

  1. No light above knee height, anywhere on the ridge.

    Path lamps point at the ground and stop at your ankles.

  2. Amber bulbs only, and few of them.

    Warm, dim glass that leaves your night vision alone.

  3. Shared lamps go off at ten, every night.

    Your own lamps stay yours, on a timer you can override.

  4. No generators, no drones, no speakers.

    The loudest thing on the ridge should be the wind.

  5. Nothing built that shows from the next ridge.

    Every stay turns its glass away from the others.

The Last
Six Miles

Every ridge is a drive from a small airport and a short stretch of gravel from the last paved road. Leave the city by lunch and you are on the deck before the light goes.

  1. 1

    Fly in small

    Reno, Las Vegas, St. George or Flagstaff, depending on the ridge. Any car will do; four-wheel drive only from December to March.

  2. 2

    Take the two-lane road

    Two to four hours of open highway. Fill the tank in the last town; we send you its name and its best pie.

  3. 3

    Six miles of gravel

    Graded, slow and beautiful. Your host meets you at the gate and brings your bags up on a cart.

No car? The shuttle leaves the last town at 2 pm on Fridays and Sundays.

Before You
Drive Out

Still unsure about something? Mara answers the phone from eight to eight, every day.

+1 760 000 0000
Is there wifi or phone signal?

Not in the stays. Each ridge has a small cabin at the gate with wifi and a landline for emergencies, and your host is always reachable.

What happens if it is cloudy?

Clouds come and go fast up here. If a whole stay is overcast, tell us before checkout and we give you a free night to use within a year.

Are the stays warm in winter?

Yes. Cabins and suites have wood stoves, domes and pods have quiet electric heating and heavy duvets, and every bed has a hot water bottle.

Can we bring children or a dog?

Children are welcome in the canvas suites at Juniper Mesa. Dogs can stay in the Rim Cabins at Snowline for $30 a night.

Is there running water and a real toilet?

Every stay has its own bathroom with a hot shower and a flushing toilet. Water comes from the ridge's own well and is safe to drink.

What is the cancellation policy?

Cancel up to fourteen days before arrival for a full refund. After that we move your dates once, free, within the next twelve months.
