What you carry

The ridge stays above 2,400 metres for sixteen kilometres. In June it can be 24 °C in Orvalle and 2 °C on Kessel Tor. Everything here is checked at the start and can be checked again at any aid station.

  • Waterproof jacketWith a hood and taped seams
  • A warm layerLong sleeves, fleece or wool
  • Legs coveredTights or trousers down to the knee or below
  • Cap and glovesThe ridge can be cold at noon
  • One litre of waterBottles or a bladder, full at every aid station
  • Your own cupThe aid stations have no cups
  • Food for two hoursAid stations are up to 9 km apart
  • Emergency blanketAt least 1.4 × 2 metres
  • WhistleMost vests have one on the chest strap
  • PhoneCharged, with the race office number saved

The finish closes at last light. The long table opens.

Orvalle has 640 people and one main street. On race night it lays a table down the whole of it.

The finish arch stands in front of the church. From seven in the evening 1,900 seats run from the square to the bridge: soup from the school kitchen, bread from both bakers, and the cheers get louder as the light goes, because the last runners in are the ones the whole street has waited for.

  • Long table from 19:00
  • Last runner, loudest cheer
  • Band until 01:00

Getting a place

Places are drawn by ballot. Everyone who applies in January has the same chance, whatever their time or their country.

  1. 11–31 JanuaryThe ballot is open. Apply alone or as a group of up to four.
  2. 8 FebruaryThe draw, in public, in the Orvalle school hall.
  3. By 1 MarchConfirm your place and pay the fee.
  4. By 15 AprilBook your trail day in May.

Oneday

Every place at SOLSTA costs one day of trail work in May.

The race uses 58 km of path that the valley keeps. So every runner gives a day back: resetting steps, clearing the scree path, cutting back the larch wood. Lunch is on us.

  1. 01Book a dayAny Saturday in May, or two weekday mornings.
  2. 02Work with the trail crew07:30 to 15:30, tools and gloves provided.
  3. 03Collect your bibHanded over at the end of the day, by the crew.

Before you apply

The questions the race office hears most, answered once.

Something else? Ask the office

Do I need a qualifying race?

No. We ask Long runners to have finished one mountain race of 40 km or more; you tick a box and we trust you.

Are poles allowed?

Yes, in all three races. Pack them before the scree, where you will want your hands.

What if the weather turns?

The ridge has a lower route along the 2,000 m contour. If the 03:00 forecast shows storms the Long runs it; if a storm arrives during the day, marshals turn runners at the col.

What are the cut-offs?

Long: the col at 12:30, Kessel Tor at 15:40, the finish at 21:40. Half: the col at 14:00, the finish at 19:30. Vertical: Kessel Hut at 20:10.

Can I give my place away?

Once, until 1 May, through the race office. The trail day goes with the place.

How do I get to Orvalle?

Train to the valley town, then the post bus, 40 minutes. On race day extra buses run from 03:30; the village has no parking on Saturday.

Pacers or dogs?

Neither on the course. Supporters can meet you at Pra Bas, at Sella and in the square.

Where do I sleep?

Orvalle has 300 beds. The school gym opens on Friday night: mattress, shower and breakfast at 03:30 for €18.
