Thursday isRoast dayFour steps, one ticket per batch

Every batch gets a paper ticket when the green coffee is weighed, and a stamp at each step until it is bagged. The ticket stays pinned above the drum until the last bag is sold.

  1. Tuesday

    Weighed out

    Green coffee comes off the pallet in twelve-kilo lots. We pick out broken and pale beans by hand on the back table.

  2. Thursday, 7 am

    Into the drum

    The drum is charged hot and each batch takes eleven to thirteen minutes. We listen for first crack and drop every coffee by its own clock.

  3. Four minutes later

    Stirred cool

    The tray's arms turn the beans until they are cool to the hand. Fast cooling keeps the sweetness the drum built.

  4. Friday

    Bagged and dated

    Each bag gets the batch number and the roast date. The beans rest a day on the shelf, then they are yours.

Asked atThe counterFive things people ask

Can you grind for my brewer?

Yes — tell us what you brew with, from espresso to cafetière, and we grind on the spot. Whole beans keep longer, so we will always offer that first.

How long do the beans keep?

They are at their best from a few days to five weeks after the roast date on the bag. Keep them sealed, cool and out of the fridge.

Do you post beans?

Orders placed by Wednesday night go out on Friday with the new roast. UK postage is free over two bags.

Can I have a regular delivery?

Every two or four weeks, whichever coffee you like or the roaster's pick. Pause or stop it from the email we send before each roast.

Do you supply cafés and offices?

We roast for a few cafés and offices in Sheffield, delivered by bike in returnable tins. Ask at the counter or send us an email.
