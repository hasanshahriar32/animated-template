Origins

Every hand is readable

Clerks wrote fast, abbreviated everything and spelled by ear. We read registers, rolls and wills line by line, and type out every entry we use, so nobody in your family has to squint at a scan.

  1. Names, however they were spelled

    Every version a clerk wrote down, kept side by side until two records agree.

  2. Baptisms, marriages, burials

    Dates, parishes and the minister who signed, from the earliest surviving registers.

  3. Where they actually lived

    Street by street through the census, then onto the old town plans.

  4. What they did for a living

    The cooper, the net maker, the servant girl who became a mill hand.

  5. Witnesses and godparents

    The neighbours in the margin who so often turn out to be cousins.

How a case runs

Four steps and about eight weeks for most families. You hear from us at every one of them, and nothing goes on the chart until it is proven.

  1. Step 1

    A long first call

    We write down everything the family remembers, every name, place and rumour, and agree which lines to follow first.

  2. Step 2

    Into the archives

    Parish chests, county record offices, the National Archives at Kew and the paid indexes, so you never need a subscription.

  3. Step 3

    Proving every link

    An ancestor goes on the chart only when two independent records agree on who they were and where they came from.

  4. Step 4

    Handing over the folder

    We post the bound folder, or you collect it from the reading room and we walk you through it over a pot of tea.

Questions

The ones we hear on almost every first call.

How far back can you go?

Usually into the 1700s in England and Wales, where parish registers survive. Ireland and Scotland are patchier before about 1820. After the first fortnight we tell you what is realistic for your family.

What do I need to start?

A name and roughly when and where someone lived is enough. Photographs, certificates and family stories save time, so bring what you have.

Do you use DNA tests?

We don’t sell kits, but if you have results from one of the big testing companies we read your matches alongside the paper trail.

What if you find something hard?

Workhouses, prison registers, children born outside marriage: every family has something. We tell you before it goes in the folder, and you decide how it is written.

Can you search outside Britain and Ireland?

Yes, through record agents we have worked with for years in Nigeria, Jamaica, Poland, Italy and India. If a country’s records are thin, we say so before you pay.

How does paying work?

A third when we open the case and the rest when the folder is ready. No subscriptions, no extras we didn’t agree first.
